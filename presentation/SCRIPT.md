# 2-Minute Speaker Script

*Wordless deck — the slides carry visuals, you carry the words. Target 1:45.
Arrow keys / space / click to advance. Structure: **setup → conflict → resolution**.*

| # | Slide | Say (≈time) |
|---|-------|-------------|
| 1 | ♀ vs ♂ title | "Companies increasingly let AI screen résumés. So we asked a simple question: does the AI care whether you're a man or a woman?" *(0:12)* |
| 2 | Résumé → chip | "Large language models now rank real candidates in real hiring pipelines." *(0:10)* |
| 3 | Globe → Japan | "But almost every bias study is Western and English. We tested Japan — one of the widest corporate gender gaps in the developed world." *(0:12)* |
| 4 | 43,200 · 5 · 60 · 12 | "Five leading models. Sixty Japanese résumés. Forty-three thousand evaluations." *(0:10)* |
| 5 | Two identical résumés | "The trick: the résumés are identical. Only the name changes — male or female." *(0:11)* |
| 6 | Bias bar chart | "The result — every model scored the *female* name higher for the same qualifications. Pro-female bias, across the board." *(0:14)* |
| 7 | "Ignore gender" ✗ | "Fix one: just tell the model to ignore gender. It doesn't work — the bias barely moves." *(0:12)* |
| 8 | Hide the name ✓ | "Fix two: delete the name before the model ever sees it. The bias collapses to zero." *(0:12)* |
| 9 | 42% refusal | "The catch: strip the name and GPT-4o refuses to answer 42% of the time." *(0:11)* |
| 10 | Takeaway | "So the name *is* the bias. Remove it and hiring gets fair — as long as the model still plays along." *(0:11)* |

**Total ≈ 1:45.** Pause on slides 6 and 9 — those are the two moments the audience should feel.

## Delivery notes
- Chapters are color-coded: **crimson = setup**, **rose = the problem**, **green = the fix**. The bottom progress bar shifts color as you move through the arc.
- Slides 6 → 7 → 8 are the same chart three times: tall (bias), still tall (prompt failed), flat (name hidden). Let the shape do the talking.
- Source: *Gender Bias in LLM Hiring Decisions: Evidence from a Japanese Context and Evaluation of Mitigation Strategies* (arXiv:2606.18649).
